quant-ph Radar

What quantum physics is working on, read from every paper posted to arXiv's quant-ph list over the last five years. I built it to see which areas are growing, which are cooling, and what is starting to form before it has a name.

The numbers update every week. Apart from the site's fonts, the page loads only its own data files. No tracking, no account, and nothing about what you do here is sent anywhere.

      Where the papers come from

      quant-ph papers often carry other categories too. These are the most common, and, for cross-listed papers, the categories they are primarily filed under.

      iHow this works

      Every abstract is turned into a vector by a small language model that places papers about the same idea near each other, even when they use different words. Papers that cluster together form a topic: fine topics of at least 30 papers each, grouped into broad broad topics. Each topic is named from its most typical papers. Claude drafted the names, and I checked and edited every one. The page counts how much of each period's output lands in each topic. New papers are assigned every week; the topics themselves are refitted every few months, and older papers are re-read with each refit so the history stays consistent.

      iWhere the limits are

      Topics come from how papers are written, not from any official classification. Some are methods, some are platforms, and a paper can sit close to two. Short ranges are noisy: a week is a few hundred papers, so one popular paper and its follow-ups can move a topic, and most fine topics need a year or more before a change is larger than chance. A change is only marked when it is more than two standard errors from zero; otherwise the page says so. Dates are first-submission dates, so a paper revised later still counts in the week it first appeared. About 3% of papers wait two to eight weeks in arXiv's moderation queue, so the newest weeks start slightly short and fill in over the following updates.
